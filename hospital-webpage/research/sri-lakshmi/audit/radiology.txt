Is radiation used in all imaging techniques?
Not all imaging techniques use radiation. X-rays, CT scans, and fluoroscopy involve ionizing radiation, whereas MRI and ultrasound do not use ionizing radiation.
Is radiation from X-rays and CT scans dangerous?
While X-rays and CT scans involve low doses of radiation, repeated exposure may carry some risks. However, radiologists carefully consider the benefits of the procedure versus the potential risks.
Can pregnant women undergo radiological exams?
Pregnant women should inform their healthcare provider and radiologist about their pregnancy to determine the necessity and safety of any radiological exam. Some imaging procedures may be avoided or postponed during pregnancy.
How long does it take to get imaging results?
The time to receive imaging results can vary depending on the facility and urgency of the case. In emergencies, results may be available immediately, while in non-urgent cases, it may take a few days.
Is contrast dye used in all imaging studies?
Contrast dye is not used in all imaging studies. It is often used in CT scans, MRI, and some angiograms to enhance the visibility of certain structures or blood vessels.
Do radiologists perform surgeries or provide treatment?
Radiologists are primarily involved in diagnosing diseases through medical imaging. However, interventional radiologists may perform minimally invasive procedures to treat certain conditions.
Are radiologists and radiologic technologists the same?
No, radiologists are medical doctors who specialize in interpreting medical images, while radiologic technologists are skilled professionals who perform the imaging procedures but do not interpret the images.
