Key Neurology Services Include
Key Neurology Services at our hospital encompass comprehensive diagnostics, treatment, and management of neurological disorders. Our expert team utilizes advanced technology to provide personalized care for optimal patient outcomes.
Neurology Consultations:
Comprehensive evaluations by experienced neurologists to diagnose and manage a variety of neurological conditions, including headache disorders, epilepsy, stroke, movement disorders, and neuropathies.
Diagnostic Testing:
Advanced diagnostic tests, including electroencephalography (EEG), electromyography (EMG), nerve conduction studies (NCS), and neuroimaging (MRI, CT scan), to accurately assess and diagnose neurological conditions.
Stroke Care:
Rapid evaluation and treatment of stroke, including thrombolytic therapy (clot-busting drugs), mechanical thrombectomy, and neurointensive care, to minimize brain damage and improve outcomes.
Epilepsy Management:
Comprehensive management of epilepsy, including medication therapy, ketogenic diet, vagus nerve stimulation (VNS), and epilepsy surgery, to control seizures and improve quality of life.
Movement Disorders:
Specialized care for movement disorders, such as Parkinson's disease, essential tremor, dystonia, and Huntington's disease, with a focus on medication management, botulinum toxin injections, and deep brain stimulation (DBS) therapy.
Headache Clinic:
Multidisciplinary evaluation and treatment of headache disorders, including migraines, tension headaches, cluster headaches, and medication-overuse headaches, with a focus on personalized treatment plans and headache management strategies.
Neuromuscular Disorders:
Diagnosis and management of neuromuscular disorders, including peripheral neuropathies, myasthenia gravis, muscular dystrophies, and motor neuron diseases, with a focus on symptomatic treatment and rehabilitation.
Memory
Disorders: Comprehensive evaluation and management of memory disorders, including Alzheimer's disease, mild cognitive impairment (MCI), and other dementias, with a focus on cognitive testing, medication therapy, and supportive care.

What does a neurologist do?
A neurologist is a medical doctor who specializes in diagnosing and treating diseases and disorders of the nervous system. They manage conditions such as epilepsy, stroke, Superple sclerosis, movement disorders, and peripheral nerve disorders.
What are common symptoms of neurological disorders?
Common symptoms of neurological disorders may include headaches, seizures, weakness, numbness, difficulty walking, tremors, memory problems, and changes in speech or vision.
What causes neurological disorders?
Neurological disorders can have various causes, including genetics, infections, immune system disorders, trauma, tumors, and degenerative conditions.
When should I see a neurologist?
You should consider seeing a neurologist if you experience persistent or concerning neurological symptoms, such as unexplained headaches, seizures, weakness, balance problems, or changes in cognition.
Can neurological conditions be cured?
The outcome and treatment options depend on the specific neurological condition. While some conditions can be managed effectively and symptoms improved, others may require ongoing treatment and symptom management.
What is a stroke, and how is it treated?
A stroke occurs when blood flow to the brain is interrupted, leading to brain cell damage. Treatment for stroke includes time-sensitive interventions such as clot-dissolving medications (thrombolytics) and, in some cases, mechanical thrombectomy to remove the clot.
Can neurology treatment be combined with other medical specialties?
Yes, neurology often collaborates with other medical specialties, such as neurosurgery, physical medicine and rehabilitation, and psychiatry, to provide comprehensive care for patients with complex neurological conditions.
Are there ways to prevent neurological disorders?
While not all neurological disorders can be prevented, adopting a healthy lifestyle, managing chronic conditions (e.g., diabetes, hypertension), wearing helmets during activities with head injury risks, and avoiding toxins or infections that can affect the nervous system may reduce the risk of some conditions.
5.What are the potential risks of surgery?
Like any surgical procedure, laparoscopic surgery carries some risks. These risks can include bleeding, infection, damage to surrounding organs, and adverse reactions to anesthesia. However, our experienced surgical team takes all necessary precautions to minimize these risks and ensure the best possible outcome.
6.Will I have scars after laparoscopic surgery?
While laparoscopic surgery involves small incisions, there will still be some scars. However, these scars are typically much smaller and less noticeable than those from traditional open surgery. Over time, the scars tend to fade and become less visible.
7.Is laparoscopic surgery suitable for everyone?
Laparoscopic surgery may not be suitable for every patient or condition. Factors such as the patient's overall health, the complexity of the procedure, and previous abdominal surgeries may influence the decision to opt for laparoscopic surgery. Your surgeon will discuss the most appropriate treatment option based on your individual case.
8.Will I need to stay in the hospital after surgery?
The length of hospital stay depends on the specific procedure and your individual recovery progress. Many laparoscopic surgeries are performed on an outpatient basis, allowing patients to go home the same day. However, some procedures may require an overnight stay for monitoring.
