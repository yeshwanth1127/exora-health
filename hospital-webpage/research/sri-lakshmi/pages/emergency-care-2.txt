When should I call for emergency medical assistance?
Call for emergency medical assistance immediately if you encounter a life-threatening situation, such as:
-
Chest pain or suspected heart attack
-
Difficulty breathing or sudden shortness of breath
-
Unconsciousness or altered mental status
-
Severe bleeding that cannot be controlled
-
Suspected stroke (sudden numbness, weakness, or slurred speech)
-
Choking or difficulty swallowing
-
Seizures or convulsions
-
Severe allergic reactions with difficulty breathing or swelling of the face and throat
What should I do if someone around me experiences a sudden cardiac arrest?
If someone experiences a sudden cardiac arrest, immediately call for emergency medical assistance and start performing hands-only CPR until help arrives.
Is emergency care provided on a first-come, first-served basis?
No, emergency care is provided based on the severity of the patient's condition. Triage is performed to prioritize patients with life-threatening conditions to receive immediate attention.
What information should I provide to emergency responders when I call for help?
When calling for emergency medical assistance, be prepared to provide the following information:
-
Your name and location (address or landmarks)
-
The nature of the medical emergency or injury
-
The number of patients involved and their condition
-
Any specific medical conditions or known allergies of the patient
Can I visit the emergency room for non-emergency conditions?
Emergency rooms are designed to handle life-threatening and urgent medical conditions. For non-life-threatening conditions or minor injuries, it is best to visit an urgent care center or your primary care physician.
